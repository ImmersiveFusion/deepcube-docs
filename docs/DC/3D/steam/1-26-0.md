# DeepCube™ 1.26.0

Everything since 1.25.1 lands in this Steam update. Every trace, span and log block, and every service, now shows its state with a pattern and a symbol as well as a color, so you can tell Failed from Processed without telling red from green. Lost traces and Warning logs are amber, Info logs are blue, and a service with no call for five minutes is Idle, with a look of its own. Tessa says how much she checked before she says nothing is wrong. Operation counts read correctly in thousands, millions and billions.

## What's New
- Status in pattern and symbol: every block carries its state three ways, a color, a pattern on its sides and a symbol on its ends. Traces: Processed is stripes and a check, Failed a crosshatch and an X, In flight dots and a ring, Lost diagonal stripes and a slashed ring. Logs: Error is a zigzag and an X, Warning chevrons and a triangle, Info, Debug and Verbose small squares and a dot. The span and log bars in the Diagnostics cube wear the same patterns and symbols.
- Every service shows its status: each service and dependency has a status block on its platform. Failed for a service in error, or a dependency whose callers' calls are failing; Processed for a healthy service; the Processed pattern and check in grey for Idle; plain grey with a slashed ring for No data, a dependency that sends no telemetry of its own.
- Idle after five minutes: a service is Idle once it has had no call for five minutes, with its own look. Before, it went idle after 30 or 60 seconds and wore the In flight look.
- One In flight look: a received trace and a published one look the same; hover over the block, and its details still say which. A trace looks In flight from every distance while any of its spans is in flight, so up close and far away now agree.
- Amber and blue: Lost traces and Warning logs are amber at every distance, and Info logs are blue. Debug and Verbose stay grey. Seen from above, status blocks sit at 45 degrees.
- Tessa names her scope: when nothing looks wrong, she is set to say how much she checked, for example "Nothing looks wrong across the 40 services on this grid." If your access does not cover what you asked about, she is set to say so, instead of saying there is nothing there.
- Tessa says when she could not finish: a turn that ends without an answer now says so. If her service times out, the chat says "Tessa can't answer right now. Try again in a minute." A Stop you press yourself stays silent.
- Shorter grid walk-throughs: ask Tessa to walk you through the health of your grid, and she is set to keep it under about 120 words, verdict first, counting the healthy services instead of naming every one.
- Thin scrollbars: every dialog, including Help and Preferences, shows a thin scrollbar, and Help and Preferences scroll faster. The grid picker stops at the end of its list instead of springing back.
- Links in the chat: only full web addresses become links, so a file name such as main.py stays plain text.
- Security fixes.

## Fixes
- Fixed operation counts: 1,000 now shows as 1K and 20,000,000 as 20M, where they showed as 1000 and 2M.
- Fixed a service that a caller named before its first span showing as a node that was not instrumented, while it was serving traffic.
- Fixed every Tessa answer starting with blank lines.
- Fixed the Thinking row staying in the chat after Tessa's turn had ended.
- Fixed the chat offering /tessa-check, a developer test, to customers.

## Known Issues
- A dependency's numbers and error state can differ between DeepCube and DeepCube Web.
- Demo mode hides only the grid's name. Hosts, URLs and database names are shown as your services report them. Check what is visible before you demo or share your screen.
- macOS support is experimental and may lack full feature parity with Windows.
